Our Vision

Pakistan's agricultural sector accounts for roughly ninety percent of national freshwater withdrawal, yet farmers and planners often lack timely district-level visibility into how much rainfall a coming month is likely to deliver, and whether it will meet the demand of the crops they have planted. This dashboard exists to close that gap with a transparent, auditable, decision-grade forecast.

Data

Nine years of historical weather, crop, and soil records harmonised into a single time-aware dataset.

Method

A gradient-boosting rainfall model combined with FAO Paper 56 crop coefficients and a soil retention factor.

Access

Built for farmers, extension officers, agricultural researchers and district-level planners.

How the index is computed

01

Predict Rainfall

A gradient-boosting regressor predicts monthly rainfall from lagged history, cyclical seasonality, temperature, humidity, wind, and solar radiation.

02

Derive Supply

Rainfall is multiplied by a soil retention factor and added to any supplemental irrigation the user reports to produce the effective water supply.

03

Compute WSI

The Water Sufficiency Index is the ratio of supply to crop water demand, expressed as a percentage and mapped to a five-level severity class.
